<section class="xc-section-shell">
  <h2>Step one: confirm product type and quantity</h2>
  <div class="xc-spec-grid">
    <div class="xc-spec-card">
      <span>Product family</span>
      <strong>Space Capsule, Apple Cabin, Wooden Cabin, or container system</strong>
      <p>Different product lines respond very differently to shipping size, handling method, and site constraints.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="xc-spec-card">
      <span>Model and configuration</span>
      <strong>Dimensions, completion level, and option scope</strong>
      <p>These factors influence packing volume, weight, and transport planning.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="xc-spec-card">
      <span>Quantity</span>
      <strong>Initial quantity and whether delivery is phased</strong>
      <p>This helps determine whether a single demonstration unit or a batch shipment is more practical.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="xc-spec-card">
      <span>Destination</span>
      <strong>Country, nearest port, and project location</strong>
      <p>This is the baseline information for judging route and shipping logic.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="xc-section-shell">
  <h2>Step two: match transport and installation to the product</h2>
  <div class="xc-feature-grid">
    <div class="xc-feature-card">
      <span>Hospitality units</span>
      <strong>Usually need earlier discussion on oversize transport, lifting, and final placement</strong>
      <p>Finished units such as capsules often need route and site planning earlier in the process.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="xc-feature-card">
      <span>Container systems</span>
      <strong>Usually support more standardized packing and repeatable shipment planning</strong>
      <p>That makes them well suited to projects prioritizing loading efficiency and repeated deployment.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="xc-feature-card">
      <span>Site preparation</span>
      <strong>Foundation work, lifting equipment, and utility access should move in parallel</strong>
      <p>If these topics are delayed until after shipment, project risk usually rises sharply.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>
