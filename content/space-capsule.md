## Why Overseas Buyers Start Here

- The product itself helps market the destination, not only provide a room.
- Capsule units fit projects that need a memorable exterior image.
- They are easier to compare by layout strategy than by generic “mobile house” terminology.
- Buyers can start with a standard model and then discuss project-specific options.

## How to Choose Between E5, E6, and E7

| Model | Typical buyer goal | Layout direction | Good starting use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| E5 | Create a premium room with strong visual appeal | Compact room with balcony-style experience | Scenic room, glamping pod, flagship resort unit |
| E6 | Keep compact length but gain more interior function | One bedroom plus living-room logic | Resort room with more indoor usability |
| E7 | Offer a larger, higher-value guest room | Bigger suite-style arrangement | Boutique hotel room, family unit, premium stay |

## Typical Use Cases

- Scenic-area room clusters
- Glamping and eco-tourism accommodation
- Resort signature rooms
- Boutique hospitality projects
- Demonstration units for dealers or destination developers

## What Buyers Usually Want to Confirm

- Which model best fits the target room count and project positioning
- Whether the project needs a more compact unit or a larger suite
- What interior options should be standardized before production
- How transport and unloading will work for the destination
- Whether local installation resources are available onsite
